Facility and method

An inspection is only as good as the conditions it was run under. This page sets out what we measure, how the vehicle is controlled, and what arrives at the end.

01

What gets measured

Five dimensions are recorded on every inspection, for the candidate part and for the genuine OEM part, in the same vehicle position under the same conditions.

Measurement set, recorded for both candidate and OEM part
Measurement What is recorded Why it matters
Gap and flush Edge-to-edge spacing and surface height difference at each defined point along every mating edge. This is what a customer sees standing at the vehicle, and the first thing a shop or insurer rejects a part for.
Hole alignment Position and clearance of every mounting hole, stud, and fastener location relative to the vehicle's captive points. A part can sit correctly on the surface and still be unfittable. Misalignment shows up as forced fasteners and stripped threads.
Bracketry and reinforcement Presence, position, gauge, and engagement of brackets, reinforcements, absorbers, and mounting tabs against the OEM part. Missing or relocated reinforcement changes how a part behaves in service and in a later collision, even when the outer surface fits.
Surface condition Coating finish, primer coverage, weld quality, edge condition, and visible defects on the part as received. Determines whether the part reaches a standard finish without preparation the shop never priced for.
Installation time Elapsed fitting time, plus any deviation from factory procedure required to seat the part. Time is the cost a shop absorbs. A part that needs rework to fit is a real expense even when the finished result passes.
02

Vehicle sourcing and baseline

The vehicle is the instrument. If its own geometry is unknown, no measurement taken from it means anything.

Donor selection

Inspections are run on undamaged donor vehicles of the correct platform, model year, and trim for the part under test. A vehicle that has been previously repaired in the area being measured is not used, since prior repair work introduces variation that cannot be separated from the part itself.

Baseline measurement

Before anything is removed, the vehicle is measured with its factory part still fitted. That baseline establishes the vehicle's own build tolerance at every point we intend to measure later, so ordinary vehicle-to-vehicle variation is never misattributed to the candidate part.

Both the OEM reference part and the candidate part are then fitted to that same vehicle, in the same position. Holding the vehicle constant is what makes the comparison meaningful.

Identification

Vehicle identification, build date, and trim level are recorded in the report alongside the part number, supplier, and any batch or lot markings present on the part as received. Every measurement in the report is traceable to a specific vehicle and a specific part.

03

Reporting and delivery

Reports are written to be read by a quality engineer and to survive being forwarded to a customer, an insurer, or a certifying body.

Report contents

Each report opens with the determination and the conditions it was reached under, then carries the full measurement tables: every recorded point, candidate against OEM, with the vehicle baseline shown alongside. Photographic evidence is indexed to the measurement points it documents rather than collected in an appendix.

Format

Delivered as a single PDF, with measurement data available separately in tabular form on request for customers who need to load results into their own quality system.

Turnaround

Target turnaround from part receipt to delivered report: [TURNAROUND TIME]. Vehicle sourcing is the variable that drives lead time on uncommon platforms, and we confirm it in writing before an inspection is scheduled.

Confidentiality

Dimensional Inspection VTF results are released only to the requesting manufacturer. Certification, complaint, and marketplace monitoring results follow the disclosure rules of the programme they belong to.
