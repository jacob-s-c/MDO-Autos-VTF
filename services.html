Inspection types

Four inspections, one measurement method. What separates them is the question being asked, who initiates them, and what the result is allowed to affect.

Summary of inspection types
Inspection Who initiates Effect on certification
Certification VTF Manufacturer or certifying body Determines whether certification is granted
Complaint VTF Repairer or shop reporting a field issue May suspend or withdraw existing certification
Marketplace Monitoring VTF MDO Autos, unannounced May trigger re-certification
Dimensional Inspection VTF Manufacturer, voluntary None. Advisory only
01

Certification VTF

The inspection a candidate part must pass before it can carry independent fit certification.

What it is

A controlled, direct comparison between a candidate part and the genuine OEM part it is intended to replace. Both are installed on the same undamaged donor vehicle, in the same position, by the same technician, using factory fastener sequence and torque.

We record gap and flush along every mating edge, hole and fastener alignment, bracket and reinforcement engagement, surface condition, and the labour time each part required. The candidate is scored against the OEM part as the physical reference standard, not against a drawing tolerance in isolation.

When it applies

Before a part enters a certification programme, and again whenever a certified part is revised: new tooling, changed material, a new production site, or any design revision affecting mating surfaces. A part that has never been measured against its OEM equivalent on an actual vehicle cannot be certified on drawings alone.

What you receive

  • Full dimensional report covering every measured point, candidate against OEM
  • Photographic record of both installations at each mating edge
  • Gap and flush readings tabulated per measurement point
  • Installation time, with any departure from factory procedure noted
  • A clear pass or fail determination against the OEM reference
  • Where a part fails, an itemised list of the non-conforming dimensions
02

Complaint VTF

A root-cause investigation opened when a shop reports that an already-certified part does not fit.

What it is

When a repairer reports a fit problem in the field, the useful question is rarely whether the part is bad. It is where the deviation originated. We re-run the certification measurements on a fresh sample of the same part, drawn from current production wherever possible, and compare the results against the readings taken when that part was originally certified.

That comparison separates three distinct causes that look identical from inside a body shop: a part that has drifted away from the certified sample, a vehicle that was already out of tolerance before work began, and an installation that departed from factory procedure.

When it applies

On receipt of a documented fit complaint against a part holding current certification. The report should identify the vehicle, the part position, and the nature of the misfit. Photographs from the reporting shop are useful but not required.

What you receive

  • Root-cause finding attributing the deviation to the part, the vehicle, or the installation
  • Side-by-side comparison against the original certification readings
  • Photographic evidence from the re-inspection
  • Assessment of whether certification should stand, be suspended, or be withdrawn
  • The corrective measurements needed to bring the part back into conformance
03

Marketplace Monitoring VTF

A spot-check on parts bought from the open market, catching quality drift between certification and shipping production.

What it is

Certification measures the sample a manufacturer submits. Marketplace monitoring measures what a repair shop actually receives. We purchase parts anonymously through ordinary distribution channels and put them through the same inspection used for certification.

The manufacturer is not told which part, which batch, or when. Because the sample is bought rather than supplied, the result reflects genuine shipping production, including any tooling wear, material substitution, or process change that has occurred since the part was certified.

When it applies

On a recurring schedule across a certified catalogue, and on demand when a part category shows a rising complaint rate. It runs without notice to the manufacturer by design, since advance warning would defeat the purpose of the check.

What you receive

  • Inspection report on the market-purchased sample
  • Direct comparison against the certification readings for the same part
  • Purchase provenance: source, date, and batch or lot markings as received
  • Drift assessment identifying which dimensions have moved since certification
  • Recommendation on whether the part warrants full re-certification
04

Dimensional Inspection VTF

A voluntary inspection a manufacturer can request at any time, with no certification decision attached.

What it is

The same measurement work as a certification inspection, run at the manufacturer's request and reported only to the manufacturer. Because no certification outcome is attached, it can be used freely during development.

Typical uses are validating first-article parts off new tooling, checking a design revision before committing to production, comparing candidate suppliers against the same OEM reference, and confirming that a corrective action has actually closed a dimensional gap. Parts are not passed or failed here; they are measured, and the numbers are reported.

When it applies

At any point in a part's life, on either a certified or a candidate part. Common triggers are first-article validation, a pre-certification rehearsal, post-corrective-action verification, and supplier comparison.

What you receive

  • Dimensional report on every measured point, candidate against OEM
  • Photographic record of the installation
  • Deviation summary showing where the part departs from the OEM reference
  • No certification determination. Results are advisory and confidential to the requester
